    About me

    Aug 2022 – present

    Wind energy & dynamic line rating

    Graduate Researcher (Ph.D. Candidate), Columbia University, New York, NY

    • Applies spatial analysis to develop a risk-mitigation framework for wind energy along transmission corridors, where the same wind that drives generation also cools the line and raises its dynamic line rating (DLR), so transfer capacity peaks just as wind output does.
    • Predicts wind speed between sparse measurement points, forecasts with calibrated confidence intervals, and culminates in sensor-placement recommendations to improve safety and reduce curtailment.

    Project page

    Aug 2022 – present

    Machine learning for air‑sea fluxes

    Graduate Researcher (Ph.D. Candidate), Columbia University, New York, NY

    • Addresses oceanographic observational data scarcity in air-sea flux representation in climate modeling by developing a hybrid physics–machine learning workflow using ensembles of neural networks trained on 3 million synthetic samples, then fine-tuned via transfer learning to ~10,000 in-situ observations.
    • Employs causal discovery methods (PCMCI+) to illuminate directional relationships between meteorological drivers and air-sea fluxes, enabling physically grounded interpretation of machine learning predictions.
